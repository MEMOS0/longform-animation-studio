2. wolf/beast companion mythology;
3. modern/sci-fi boy/comic register;
4. alien ship destruction and escape-pod backstory;

6. red-armored force;

## Cross-film doctrine for compressed mythic quests

1. Open with a concrete quest or threat within the first minute.
2. Give companions complementary functions — force, knowledge, transport, comic chaos, moral judgment — not interchangeable bodies.
3. Use episodic creature/environment encounters to reveal world rules and character psychology.
4. Tie spectacle to protection, cost, choice, or ideological conflict.
5. Let the lore object carry a moral/material cost. The flower of immortality is strongest because it grows from extinction, not because it glows.
6. Maintain a deliberate tonal contract. Comic banter can coexist with brutality if the architecture knows why.
7. Even in serial format, close a micro-objective: escape, survive, win a first confrontation, recover evidence, reach a decisive answer, or change alliance/power state.
8. A continuation hook should be a specific next story question, not a naked subscribe CTA.
9. Do not overload a 12-minute short with multiple unrelated tragedy engines unless they converge before the threshold.
