## `DnhouSFc_h4`: why the low item is evidence but not a fair low film comparator

`DnhouSFc_h4` is essentially the climax/excerpt of the second `QK` unit. Its dialogue directly matches `QK` around 47:00–52:45:

- “The core is in its head… open the head armor… fire into the core”
- “I can still feel you / I'll take my share”
- “Can't lose you too”
- “Then we only get one try”
- “Cole. My name is Cole.”

This is a crucial format lesson: the same climax, when stripped of the preceding coercion, feeding/naming, institutional betrayal, and shared-load buildup, becomes mostly spectacle. The excerpt’s low discovery cannot prove weak writing or weak CTR; it does show that climax material depends on accumulated relational context. Do not treat it as an independent film-quality comparator.

## Production doctrine: bonded machine-pilot integrity

A bonded-machine story should not begin with hardware spectacle. It should:

1. Establish ordinary economic identity before the call: rent, delivery, work, insurance, debt, family obligations.
2. Make machine compatibility selective or mutual rather than a simple military assignment.
3. Force embodied learning before competence: balance, walking, misfires, fear, near-accidents.
4. Keep a civilian relational anchor active during the mission, especially parent/family/friend/mentor contact.
5. Give the machine/creature vulnerable agency: it can be hurt, refuse, choose, protect, trust, or need help.
6. Let the institution treat the bond as property, specimen, weapon, or unauthorized asset.
7. Convert the climax into shared load and cost, not superior firepower alone.
8. End with identity beyond utility: name, personhood, freedom, family, home, or chosen companionship.
9. If packaged as a compilation, preserve a thematic object across units. Separate episodes can work when each is a complete relational arc and all remain about the same machine/bond/control question.
