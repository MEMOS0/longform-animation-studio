- **03:00–04:00** Character’s Eye awakens: hints when making a choice, usable only three times per day. The hint specifies exact system time and location for surviving the void jump.

- **10:00–20:00** other women awaken talents, fish up uneven resources, and argue over pooling supplies equally versus rewarding individual contribution.
- **20:00–25:00** system announcements expand the island, add terrain/resources, warn that chests can spawn beasts, and require at least ten survivors to remain alive. Power and survival now include population obligation.
- **45:00–50:00** hunger creates envy and transactional exchange: food, foot rubs, meat smells, and the inequality between Ye Feng’s barbecue and the group’s sour fruit/scraps.
- **60:00–70:00** rationing becomes a leadership crisis. The leader wants to preserve food for tomorrow; hungry survivors demand more now because weakness prevents gathering and firewood work.
- **70:00–75:00** choice consequences diverge: one person is eliminated/poisoned while others lose trivial items and gain food/water. Randomness is legible but morally unstable.

### Gendered transaction is strong drama but ethically risky

- **50:00–60:00** a performer asks what she must offer for food, frames intimacy/heartbeat as proof, and turns survival inequality into a “deal.”
- **90:00–95:00** group members debate dignity versus joining Ye Feng; one warns that joining him means becoming “his dogs.”
- **115:00–end** a woman threatens to expose how much food he has unless he feeds her; he reverses the threat, she renounces pride/class status, and he negotiates beast-killing payment for treasure.
- The medium film has a powerful social engine, but much of it depends on gendered transactional leverage. This can generate compulsive conflict while narrowing audience identification and creating ethical discomfort. It should not be copied as simple wish fulfillment.

### Ending: hook is interpersonal/power-based rather than ecological

The final minutes do not resolve the survival game. They close a negotiation phase—food exposure threat, apology, beast-killing deal—and open a chest/treasure hook. Compared with the hit’s firewood/storm/Titanoboa ecological chain, the hook is more about dominance, class inversion, and transactional loyalty.

### Low-performance hypothesis: system richness outpaces relational tracking

This is not a weak film. Its underwater visuals, creature design, interfaces, and procedural imagination are strong. The discovery gap is better explained cautiously as a hypothesis:

1. The hit organizes power around one dominant object: fishing/resource reversal among classmates.
2. The medium organizes power around one dominant object: choice/hint/ration politics among a fixed gendered group.
3. The low film accumulates crafting, corruption, curses, cults, totems, sanity, island levels, evaluators, breeding, dream identities, deities, and holy items. Each is concrete, but the viewer must track too many interacting rule systems before a stable emotional relationship can carry them.

This is a cognitive-load doctrine, not proof of failure causality.

## Cross-bucket doctrine

1. **System activation should answer an injustice.** The hit’s false accusation/expulsion and the medium’s gendered expulsion create immediate moral asymmetry. The low film begins with a system screw-over, but it is less socially personal.
2. **One dominant progression object beats simultaneous system sprawl.** Fishing/resources, choice/hints/rations, or crafting/corruption can each work. Stacking too many currencies, curses, factions, and interfaces at once dilutes identification.
3. **Cheat power must be bounded.** Exact uses per day, cooldowns, stamina/HP, corruption, random chest risk, sanity drain, and scarcity turn power into decisions.
4. **Resources need visible proof.** Fish on shore, water cases, food portions, barbecue smell, cave shelter, chest contents, tools, ore, and inventory slots make progression concrete.
5. **Social order is the retention engine.** Classmates reversing hierarchy, ration disputes, pooling versus contribution, loyalty, blackmail, camp gatekeeping, and joining/leaving groups are more legible than lore alone.
6. **Power should create obligations.** Feeding followers, sheltering former enemies, keeping ten survivors alive, protecting a camp, or maintaining an island makes progression relational rather than purely masturbatory domination.
7. **Ecological threats need procedural logic.** Titanoboa habitat, storms, firewood, contamination, night demons, corruption, and predator behavior work when characters observe, infer, prepare, and adapt.
8. **Progression endings can remain open.** A total ending is not mandatory in this format. The stronger pattern is local loop closure—resource stock, plan, alliance, cure path, reward, or shelter—followed by a concrete next threat.
9. **Gendered coercion is high-risk material.** Food-for-intimacy, “men versus women,” or survival leverage must be treated as violation/conflict with consequences, not romantic reward. It may produce attention but also narrows and damages the story’s ethical center.
10. **Low discovery is not weak-craft proof.** The low film has excellent polish and dense invention. Discovery may be affected by title/thumbnail framing, runtime, audience expectation, release timing, recommendation surfaces, or cognitive accessibility.

## Production integration

Use `progression_system_survival_integrity` for system/quest/talent/level-up survival stories. It warns when system power is arbitrary rather than injustice-triggered, rules lack quantified costs, survival needs are abstract, resources are not procedural, cheat knowledge is unbounded, group politics do not feed back into survival, power creates no obligations, threats do not escalate ecologically/institutionally, gendered coercion is rewarded, or a phase ends without closing a local loop before the next hook.
