## Doctrine: coerced hidden identity must end in free re-choice, not romantic absorption

A coerced-marriage hidden-identity melodrama can become ethically and dramatically stronger when the story treats the original coercion and later identity lie as distinct wounds requiring repair:

1. **Coercion has a concrete leverage object.** Here Mara is forced through information about missing children, not vague family pressure.
2. **The disguised partner is not passive wealth.** Ethan's scar/poverty performance is connected to prior assault and self-protection, so disguise is both deception and trauma adaptation.
3. **Attachment forms before revelation.** Mara accepts practical partnership, children, labor, and household constraints before knowing status.
4. **Children create stakes but do not magically consent for adults.** Leo and Lily can choose emotional closeness, but the adult relationship still requires truth and re-choice.
5. **Procedural evidence matters.** Complaints, footage, workplace review, attorney presence, clinic records, payment trails, maternity tests, and arrests convert melodrama accusations into verifiable consequences.
6. **Money cannot erase the wound.** Property/marital assets are not accepted as automatic apology; “money was never the wound” is the key line.
7. **Anger is not signed away.** Mara can choose the relationship without pretending coercion did not happen.
8. **The final vow is a re-choice.** “Partner, not rescuer” transforms the initial forced marriage into chosen partnership only after truth, accountability, and control transfer.
9. **Consent language can be playful only after ethics are secured.** “She said yes. We checked.” works because the film has already established no-force and truth conditions.
10. **Strength is a social wound, not just a power fantasy.** Mara's unusual strength creates fear of monstrousness and rejection, making the hidden-identity symmetry mutual: both partners hide parts of themselves to survive judgment.

## Production cautions

- Do not use forced marriage as a cute premise unless the script remembers it as a coercion wound. The ending must not erase anger through wealth or wedding spectacle.
- Do not let “she accepts the poor man” alone prove virtue. The stronger proof is mutual truth after power is exposed.
- Do not treat child preference as adult consent. Children may increase emotional stakes, but the marriage requires adult re-choice.
- Do not make property transfer a transactional payoff unless it functions as restored agency/control rather than a bribe.
- Do not count duplicate uploads as independent validation of writing quality. Use them to compare packaging/distribution only.

## Integration implications

- Extend hidden-identity evaluation with a coerced-origin/free-re-choice subtype: concrete coercion leverage, trauma/function of disguise, attachment before reveal, procedural truth chain, refusal of money-as-forgiveness, restored agency/control, no-force final re-choice.
- Dialogue doctrine addendum: use specificity of refusal (“I am not signing away my anger”) rather than generic forgiveness.
- Packaging doctrine addendum: poor-dad/hidden-power thumbnails can be accurate without exposing the ending's consent ethics; test whether adding visual evidence of truth/choice would strengthen differentiation.
