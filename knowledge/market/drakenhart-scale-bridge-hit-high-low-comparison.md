## Hit mechanism: scale difference becomes stewardship, not spectacle

The tiny-kingdom hit opens with exploration procedure rather than generic wonder: cave access, Triassic plants older than dinosaurs, helmet light, field notes, drilling/report context, and the protagonist's scientific role. Sampled opening contact sheets move quickly from surface research into tactile cave discovery: bioluminescent roots, giant insect/creature scale reversal, then a miniature built civilization with guards, lights, woven infrastructure, and social hierarchy.

The middle makes scale materially dangerous. Tiny people say “FLOOD. TO THE HIGH GROUND,” and later “GIANTS BRING FLOODS every time.” The human is not merely large; her movement and the prior drilling/report economy create ecological consequences. The one-use return mechanism creates a concrete cost: “That's all I need to go home.”

Crucially, the ending is not extraction. At `00:24:50–00:24:53`, when asked why she goes back despite uncertain return, she answers: “Because I promised them.” The resolution converts scientific exploration into ongoing stewardship:

- `00:26:24–00:26:27`: “No more floods.” / “We build smarter now.”
- `00:27:15`: “This is my home now.”
- `00:27:24`: “Finish the maps I couldn't.”
- `00:28:10–00:28:16`: “You built this with us.” / “You built it.” / “I just brought the bottle caps.”
- `00:28:22–00:28:25`: “Will you come back?” / “Every sunrise I can.”

The final contact-sheet sample confirms the relational scale-bridge visually: human and tiny child face each other at the same emotional eye level, the human laughs with the child, and the final tree/hill image reframes the hidden kingdom as a continuing home rather than a conquered spectacle.

## High comparator: familiar fairy-tale inversion is strong but less systemically novel

The Cinderella comparator also performs strongly. Its opening compresses the fairy-tale contract: no beautiful clothes, magical transformation, carriage, palace, and glass slipper. Contact sheets show high-polish fairy-tale spectacle — pumpkin carriage, magical dress transformation, ballroom, glass slipper, theft/abduction, confrontation.

Its distinctive hook is not merely Cinderella but reciprocal rescue followed by betrayal: Clara helps an injured stranger on the way to the palace, he rides with her, and later the stranger steals the slipper/identity. The ending restores the object and relationship: “These belong to you” / “They were always yours,” followed by wedding/applause. This is strong craft, but it operates closer to object-restitution/identity romance than the tiny-kingdom hit's ecological world-system.

## Low comparator: creature royalty rescue is coherent but crowded and compressed

The phoenix low comparator is not proof of weak writing. It opens with immediate kidnapping/violence and protective flight: “You stole my rare bird,” “STOP OR I'LL KILL HER,” then the rescuer and child must relocate because thugs will return. The middle has a clear transformation rule: only a fire phoenix can take human form while young; the mother figure warns the child not to tell anyone until identity is understood. The ending exposes royal lineage through wartime exposition: Queen Aveline laid an egg in secret, died, and the child is High Princess. They escape to a secret refuge until the young queen is grown.

Why lower discovery may occur (hypothesis only): the compressed 13-minute runtime forces kidnapping, migration, transformation, royal exposition, enemy pursuit, and refuge setup into one continuous chain. The ending is a continuation setup rather than a rebuilt community. The emotional core is strong protection/care, but compared with the tiny-kingdom hit the world has less tactile infrastructure and less reciprocal repair.

## Production doctrine: scale-bridge stewardship

1. Scale difference must be physical, spatial, and social — not just a big/small visual gag.
2. The outsider's arrival must create material ecological or infrastructural consequences.
3. The micro-civilization needs labor, infrastructure, governance, defense, rules, and resource practices.
4. Outsider expertise should matter but remain limited; avoid instant-savior chosen-one logic.
5. Both worlds must exchange objects/knowledge/practices. Bottle caps, maps, reports, tools, seeds, or techniques should change both sides.
6. Travel/power requires scale-specific cost and limits — one-use transport, flood risk, crushing, fragility, time, energy.
7. Return/stay decisions should be relational and duty-driven, not merely logistical.
8. The ending should rebuild shared infrastructure or establish ongoing stewardship rather than escape alone.
9. Wonder is strongest when grounded in tactile objects and systems that an animation production team can design repeatedly.
