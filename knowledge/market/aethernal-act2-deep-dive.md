## Repeatable breakout hypotheses

Not causal claims:

1. Concrete threat-event title (“Then Came The Rot”) beats abstract lore in immediate legibility.
2. 19.8-minute compression lowers commitment friction versus Act I’s 33.8 minutes.
3. Ordinary survival systems (potato peels, unpaid labor, beasts, low well, water, doors) make fantasy loss intelligible.
4. False-alarm comedy builds community and dramatic irony.
5. Animal companion detection creates emotional bridge and care after spectacle.
6. Creature design stays readable across forest, village, thumbnail, and aftermath.
7. Sequel packaging is not automatically fatal: Act II outperformed Act I.

## Production lessons

- Do not open fantasy with lore councils or abstract destiny. Open with physical evidence of danger, then root the world in work/resources.
- Give the protagonist an ordinary obligation that conflicts with heroic self-image.
- Use animals/children/workers as detection and care systems.
- Let comedy arise from communal risk-assessment failure.
- Discover monster weakness through action under pressure.
- Budget loudness: attack may spike, but aftermath must become quiet enough for care and consequence.
- A sequel hook is acceptable when immediate communal survival/care lands first.
