## Doctrine update
1. **A strong hook is necessary but not sufficient even when genuinely strong.** This row falsifies any simple hook-quality model.
2. **Moral complexity must still resolve relationally.** “Revenge or savior” is excellent, but the final emotional answer must be dramatized clearly before cosmic expansion.
3. **Hidden-care objects can be powerful.** Shells reclassify god/authority as personal care; use this mechanism.
4. **Extraction metaphors travel.** “Saint as pump” is adjacent to creature-as-treasure and blood-as-key.
5. **Infrastructure dilemmas are promising but risky.** If the system powering water/food/storm safety collapses, the ending must show how vulnerable people survive, not just that the villain lost.
6. **Do not equate thumbnail beauty with clarity.** The most clickable image is not necessarily the most polished romantic fantasy tableau; it is the one that makes the specific moral question legible.

## Production use
When designing saint/sacrifice/sea-god material:
- open with serial extraction, but keep one personal care object visible;
- make hidden care evidence recur before identity revelations;
- if infrastructure depends on exploitation, the ending must replace the infrastructure or show community survival;
- do not move to “other worlds/stories beginning” until the central bond and home/community consequence have landed.
