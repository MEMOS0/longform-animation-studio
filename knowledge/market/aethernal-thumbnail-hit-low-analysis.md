# Aethernal Thumbnail Hit/Low Analysis

Evidence files live in `thumbnail_intelligence/reference_library/aethernal_channel/` with metadata linkage. The montage is `aethernal_hit_medium_low_montage.jpg`. These are visual-mechanism observations, not CTR claims.

## Hit: `31-p_UcMgOs` — Act II, about 3.6M views

Visual mechanics:

- Readable ordinary vulnerability: a young boy in simple village clothing holds a rough wooden water bucket.
- Animal companion: the large dog occupies the foreground and creates an immediate care/protection relationship.
- Approaching threat: pale rotting humanoids advance from the right/background into village space.
- Environmental clarity: thatched roofs and mud communicate world and stakes without lore text.
- Directional composition: vulnerable boy/dog on the left/center, threat entering from the right; the frame reads as “they are coming for us.”
- Franchise text exists but does not carry the image. “The Aethernal” is large, yet the picture already communicates the dramatic situation.

## Medium: `D5XuYx6HY-8` — Act I, about 529k views

Visual mechanics:

- Strong craft: hooded corrupted figure, axe-bearing warrior, cool forest depth.
- More mysterious and lore-driven: two fantasy figures are visible, but not a clearly vulnerable ordinary person or immediate attack vector.
- The corrupted hand is intriguing, but relationship and stakes are less concrete than boy/dog/village-under-attack.

Hypothesis: strong packaging, but its promise is “dark fantasy mystery” rather than “ordinary community about to be overrun.” Do not treat the difference as CTR causality.

## Low: `4mAMvbbSj3M` — Tyris teaser, about 5k views

Visual mechanics:

- Attractive armored/hooded face close-up with clean dark background.
- Large invented title plus explicit “Teaser.”
- No visible threat, relationship, object of survival, or complete-story promise.

Hypothesis: portrait quality alone is insufficient when packaging asks viewers to care about an unknown franchise/teaser rather than a dramatic situation.

## Low: `YTWjwAVniV8` — soundtrack, about 7.2k views

Visual mechanics:

- Polished dragon on treasure hoard with strong gold/red fantasy appeal.
- Text clearly sells “Soundtrack Remastered,” not a story.
- The image is attractive but narratively static; there is no vulnerable human, threat direction, or unresolved relationship.

## Doctrine absorbed into Thumbnail Intelligence

1. For dark fantasy/creature threat, prefer ordinary vulnerable foreground + companion/care anchor + approaching creature threat over generic heroic portrait.
2. A strong franchise logo cannot rescue a frame that lacks dramatic direction.
3. Teaser/portrait branding is a separate contract and should not be used as the model for complete-film packaging.
4. Beautiful creature imagery can still be non-narrative if it does not show who is endangered, who cares, or what is about to happen.
5. Series labeling is a friction hypothesis, not a universal failure rule: Act II’s concrete threat-event packaging outperformed Act I.
