## Cross-tier doctrine

1. **A comic deception needs a material motive.** Surgery money and debt repayment are stronger than mischief. The audience must know why the protagonist accepts morally dirty work.
2. **Show the method.** Pregnancy report, chicken blood, practiced acting, negotiation, payment, contract term. Professional legibility makes the deception pleasurable rather than arbitrary.
3. **The target must have agency.** Elliot plays along and uses the sabotage to escape coercion; he is not merely fooled. Later he chooses not to exploit the arrangement.
4. **Pretense must remain bounded.** Contract length, compensation, exit, divorce discussion, and board/business stakes keep the deception from becoming unlimited coercion.
5. **The decisive proof of love is non-coercion under rejection.** “She doesn't owe me a thing” is the film's central ethical turn: he keeps the business deal and sends experts even when he believes she may not choose him.
6. **Corporate/family crime supplies stakes but must not consume the romance.** Murder, inheritance, board shares, arrests, and corporate leverage work best when they clarify why trust is difficult, not when they bury the emotional contract.
7. **Ending must replace performance with voluntary truth.** The fake marriage cannot simply continue; the characters must name fear, test coercion, apologize, and choose freely.
