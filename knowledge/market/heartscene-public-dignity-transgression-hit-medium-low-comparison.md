This does not prove that focus caused hit performance. It does mean the hit/medium pair offer a much more compressible packaging promise: one public act, one wrong victim, one moral consequence chain.

## Reusable production doctrine

1. Open or cold-open inside the social detonation when possible; then rewind only if the rewind deepens values rather than delaying the hook.
2. The transgression should be public, status-based, and dignity-targeting, not random violence.
3. Give the target a chance to explain or verify before escalation. Refusal to verify is part of the transgressor's moral failure.
4. Distribute responsibility: bystanders, friends, security, and family members who enable or fail to verify must be confronted.
5. The authority figure's response should be a proportionate boundary — ending engagement, refusing trust, documenting complaint — not automatically revenge or destruction.
6. Victims need safety, care, documentation, payment/labor protection, and choice, not just apology.
7. Helpers need independent competence and material stakes. Compassion should cost something real.
8. Use independent evidence — cameras, records, documents, testimony — to control truth, but preserve time for emotional aftermath.
9. In serial format, close a micro-objective, then leave specific procedural hooks such as footage circulation or contract review.
10. Do not confuse a polished low comparator with weak writing; diffusion can coexist with strong dialogue, ethical repair, and procedural justice.
11. For standalone films, do not end on musical thesis alone. Dramatize consequence or boundary after evidence emerges.
