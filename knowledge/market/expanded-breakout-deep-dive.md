## Cross-breakout synthesis — second pass

1. **Relational scale remains the strongest creature-fantasy pattern.** Giant Father and Aethernal both show vulnerable child/animal/protector relationships before spectacle.
2. **Misunderstood protector is stronger than monster spectacle.** The giant is visually frightening but behaviorally parental; the dog/wolf is protective; rot creatures are hostile because they threaten the child/community.
3. **Grounded humor can increase attachment.** Aethernal’s potato peels/chores and Nana’s orange-juice price shock make fantasy/melodrama socially concrete.
4. **Agency differentiates stronger transformation stories.** Ava designs the beggar test; Ravena refuses victimhood immediately. Passive suffering is not the only path.
5. **Social tests are powerful packaging engines.** “Who cares when you look poor?” is instantly understandable and generates episodic encounter structure.
6. **Compressed duration can outperform.** Giant Father is only ~12 minutes. Format rules must therefore remain channel- and premise-dependent; completeness/emotional density matter more than runtime alone.
7. **Franchise amplification is real but not sufficient.** Aethernal benefits from Act I, yet Act II still opens through embodied village/child/dog/threat rather than continuity recap.

## Production implications

- Build creature fantasy around misunderstood protectors and vulnerable dependents, not creature coolness.
- For transformation melodrama, give the wounded protagonist an active test, plan, or competence inversion.
- Use grounded mundane details to make fantastic stakes emotionally believable.
- Consider short complete mythic films when the premise is singular and visually dense; do not pad to 40 minutes by default.
- Thumbnail must show the relational triangle, not just genre iconography.
