## Cross-bucket doctrine

1. **The cure must begin as two-sided need.** Hit: mother miracle + king death deadline. Medium: identity/archives + control of shifting. Low: income/profession + curse management.
2. **Touch needs rules before pleasure.** The audience should know what happens when the wrong person touches, what control costs, and why this person is an exception.
3. **Consent pacing is part of the fantasy.** “No rush,” bounded care instructions, stop/pressure negotiation, and role separation make dangerous touch emotionally safe.
4. **Control must be procedural.** Breath, repeated practice, medicine verification, pressure calibration, witnessed tests, and rituals create watchable competence.
5. **Instrumental origin must become a dramatic wound.** “You used me as a cure” is not a problem to hide; it is the necessary third-act objection.
6. **The curse must be weaponizable.** Strong plots let antagonists exploit rumors, medicine, veins, contracts, family succession, or social disgust rather than merely attack with force.
7. **Love must survive loss of utility.** The decisive proof is risk/choice after the cure frame is questioned or no longer sufficient.
8. **The ending must exceed curse removal.** Family road/home, archives/identity justice, shared rule, or independent studio/livelihood are stronger closures than “the curse is gone.”
9. **Low discovery can coexist with strong craft.** The Medusa low film is a counterexample against simplistic failure rules.

## Production integration

Use `curse_cure_intimacy_integrity` when a story combines curse/dangerous touch/transformation, creature or royal coding, and intimacy/care. It warns when the cure person lacks independent motive, consent/pacing is skipped, control is instant, instrumental use is never confronted, antagonists do not weaponize the curse, love is not tested beyond utility, or the ending removes the curse without building autonomous shared life.
