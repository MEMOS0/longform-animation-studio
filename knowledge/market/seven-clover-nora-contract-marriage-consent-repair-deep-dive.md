## Core doctrine: contract romance survives only when non-retaliation replaces leverage

The film's most portable mechanism is not “fake pregnancy” or “contract marriage.” It is a staged sequence of consent stress tests:

1. **Economic desperation explains but does not erase coercion.** Nora accepts because surgery money is urgent, and she names the absence of choice.
2. **Transactional intimacy must be bounded.** “I sell my skills, not my body” is the ethical floor, but the story then tests how performance invades privacy.
3. **Competence must exceed performance.** Nora is not just an actress: musical memory, painting reproduction, jewelry/material appraisal, observation, corporate reform, and strategic planning repeatedly prove capability.
4. **Identity proof should be procedural.** DNA safe, share documents, recordings, investigation, board verification, and police handoff carry public truth.
5. **The victim must own the reversal.** Elliot's best moment is stepping back: “Let her have her revenge. We'll step in if she needs us.”
6. **Family approval is not consent.** Mrs. Prescott's acceptance, Grandpa's support, and adoptive-parent gratitude cannot substitute for Nora's choice.
7. **Persistence is dangerous unless tested against power.** Elliot's gift/persistence strategy only becomes acceptable after he proves rejection will not trigger corporate retaliation.
8. **Non-retaliation is the decisive repair.** “Liking her is my problem. She doesn't owe me a thing” is the cleanest statement of the doctrine.
9. **Final choice must be present-tense and informed.** Nora chooses after knowing the truth, regaining identity/resources, watching his behavior under rejection, and asking directly whether he likes her.
10. **The contract must end emotionally even if the marriage continues legally.** The important transition is not divorce paperwork; it is the replacement of leverage with freely chosen partnership.

## Craft weaknesses and counterexamples

- The story sometimes romanticizes manipulation before repairing it: Elliot engineers “bad cop” family pressure, uses the contract to delay divorce, and involves the adoptive parents' gratitude. These are not minor blemishes; they are exactly what the later non-retaliation test must overcome.
- Nora apologizes for being selfish even though her caution is rational after murder, replacement, and betrayal. This risks shifting too much blame onto the victim.
- The childhood promise (“I pestered her until she agreed”) is a dangerous model if copied literally. The durable mechanism is not persistence itself but the later proof that refusal is safe.
- Memory recovery through concussion is convenient. The stronger evidence chain is embodied skill + authorship + procedural documents; do not rely on amnesia alone.
- The title's pillow/blind-date claims are inaccurate or marginal. The actual engine is contract marriage + amnesiac heiress + imposter/boardroom identity proof.
