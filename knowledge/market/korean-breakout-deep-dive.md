## Cross-market synthesis

1. **Small channels can break out through niche precision, not broad appeal.** BL, royal regression, senior revenge, and historical time-slip each speak to a trained audience.
2. **Episode length is not directly comparable to feature length.** A 5–12 minute episode is optimized for serialized continuation and rapid premise reinforcement, not complete-film payoff.
3. **The strongest Korean candidate uses hybrid novelty.** `C0xQApgVtR8` combines war, time-slip/anachronism, comedy, and competent-protagonist mythology.
4. **Injustice remains portable, but intimacy can replace it in BL.** Surveillance/coercion/secret longing/power imbalance are the emotional engines.
5. **Low subscriber count does not mean weak packaging.** These videos often use high-frequency faces, direct subtitles, and immediately recognizable genre signals.

## Production implications for our studio

- If we make broad cinematic creature fantasy, do not copy microdrama pacing wholesale.
- If we make serialized romance/regression, adopt microdrama mechanics deliberately: early trope declaration, dense face emotion, episode cliffhanger, high-frequency release.
- Small-channel breakout candidates are useful because they reveal demand before channel momentum distorts results.
- We should track rolling-window candidates weekly and classify them by niche rather than pooling them into one “AI drama” bucket.
