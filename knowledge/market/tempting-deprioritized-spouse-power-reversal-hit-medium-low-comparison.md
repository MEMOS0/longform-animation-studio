## Cross-tier mechanism

1. **Deprioritization must be repeated or materially costly.** The strongest scenes are not jealousy abstractions: eight divorces, premature labor, ignored injuries, wedding/banquet displacement, protection reassignment, ring disposal.
2. **The rival needs leverage.** Gia's father's life-debt, Mia's resemblance/instability, adopted-sister/first-lover bonds, or family obligation makes the husband's failure structurally intelligible while still wrong.
3. **The boundary must be behavioral.** Cook it yourself; no ninth divorce; throw the ring away; walk back into the banquet; refuse a remarriage script.
4. **Power reversal needs competence or institution.** Texas guns/fighting plus child protection; authority over half the Greco family; audit ledgers and chief-consigliere appointment.
5. **Late regret is not access.** Leonardo's desperation and Julian's exhaustion do not reopen the relationship automatically. The hit differs because Marcello's confession is paired with ongoing chosen-family labor rather than a purely late regret script.
6. **Endings diverge:** hit chooses family, medium chooses irreversible self-rule, low chooses institutional sovereignty. All are valid, but the hit has broader emotional bandwidth because comedy, child attachment, romance, and power reversal coexist.
