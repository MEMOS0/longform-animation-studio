## Packaging and thumbnail contract

The maxres thumbnail shows a giant holding a small dragon up by the body while roaring, with a distressed woman reaching toward it. `THE MOTHER'S DRAGON 2` is huge, gold, and mobile-readable. The contract is direct: a vulnerable baby dragon is physically threatened by a giant, and a mother-like woman wants to save it.

The opening does pay this contract quickly: giants, a wounded/protective adult dragon, an egg, capture pressure, and pursuit all appear within the first minutes. The middle, however, becomes quieter and more economic than the thumbnail suggests: coal scarcity, winter survival, stable labor, feeding, naming, and public scapegoating. The thumbnail sells threat; retention likely depends on whether the audience accepts the quieter care contract after the opening action. That is a hypothesis, not measured retention.

## Production-system doctrine candidates

1. Heat/cold scarcity is a strong creature-economic engine because survival is bodily, repetitive, and public.
2. A creature born gray/weak/cursed creates immediate discrimination stakes before power or beauty.
3. Name the creature through care behavior or metaphor only after feeding/warming/breathing have been shown.
4. Quiet domestic care blocks can follow action if they contain urgent micro-goals: keep breathing, keep warm, feed, hide, work, name.
5. The state becomes frightening when it monopolizes heat and frames hoarding as civic survival.
6. Execution spectacle works better when the audience has already watched the creature eat, shiver, sleep, help, and trust.
7. “Don't kill them” restraint rules make large-scale conflict morally richer than extermination fantasy.
8. Climactic creature scale should convert into embrace/recognition/protection, not only attack power.
9. A sequel hook may leave outer survival uncertain, but the central abandonment wound still needs a recognition line or protective image before the hook.
10. Sound design should exploit contrast: loud public violence, then close breath/contact/labor, then loud ritual spectacle, then fragile creature vocalization.
