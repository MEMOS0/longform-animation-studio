## Cross-tier doctrine

1. **Coercive betrayal must be concrete.** GPS bracelet, frozen passport/accounts, serial abductions, withheld medical care, `$100` transfers, ignored voice messages, forced family rules.
2. **Exit must be logistical.** Move the passport, cancel the engagement, call a lawyer, accept the divorce, walk away before the first meeting, or remove access rather than merely cry.
3. **Evidence and institutions matter.** Lawyers, trials, prison, transfers, documents, medical records, packaged evidence. Revenge spectacle alone is less durable.
4. **Regret and sacrifice do not purchase access.** Damian can sacrifice his life and still remain outside Scarlett's romantic future; Alexander can regret everything and still lose the privilege to touch Claire; Adrian can die spiritually and still not be chosen in the reset.
5. **Restitution is not reconciliation.** Damian's equity transfer and anonymous trust are meaningful because they do not require Scarlett's signature or intimacy.
6. **Replacement intimacy must be careful.** Daniel's patience, attention to medication/food/healing, explicit question, and non-pressure contrast with coercive possession.
7. **Do not aestheticize self-destruction as justice.** The low film's gun sequence is powerful but ethically risky. Professional originals should prefer institutional accountability, repair, refusal, and survivor-centered consequences.
