## Timestamped mechanism evidence

### `PyZn_Y62Cw4` — localized family persecution engine, no fantasy required

- **00:02–00:20:** A daughter-in-law denies food to hungry elderly parents-in-law, orders courtyard cleaning, and threatens eviction if the son is told.
- **00:30–02:20:** The abuse is concrete and repetitive: food humiliation, washing clothes, illness, denied medicine, physical pain, cold nights, forced labor.
- **03:04–04:23:** The son arrives; the parents hide the abuse to protect his marriage. His direct observations — weak father, cracked hands, hunger — create dramatic irony and suspicion.
- **20:00–23:20:** Final contact-sheet imagery moves through public confrontation, crying/remorse, elder care, and family reconciliation imagery.

Working interpretation: this hit does not use fantasy, child heir, royalty, dragon, or hidden identity. Its engine is instantly legible domestic injustice plus protective concealment, family loyalty, and moral discovery. The audience understands the stakes within seconds because the victim, oppressor, cost, and concealment are physically visible. This supports the broader rule “legible injustice + relational protection + moral reversal” rather than the narrower rule “child/fantasy is required.”

Important counterexample to production-quality assumptions: the subtitles include an explicit like/subscribe interruption around **00:58–01:06**, yet the video still performed extremely well. This does not make interruptions desirable; it means strong localized emotional stakes can overcome artifacts that might hurt weaker openings.

## Cross-channel preliminary synthesis

1. **Legible injustice travels across genres.** Elder abuse, execution for intelligence, rejected creature, social humiliation, birth under attack, and consent violation all create rapid emotional orientation.
2. **Fantasy is optional; emotional mechanics are not.** The Urdu family hit and wedding drama show strong demand without mythology.
3. **Protection must be credible and morally bounded.** The strongest openings attach vulnerability to a protector/agentive choice: parents protecting a son’s marriage, protagonist protecting a creature, grandfather/monk protecting a newborn, heroine helping an old woman.
4. **Fast agency separates competence fantasies from suffering loops.** `bHCI5XUSppM` moves from execution to money-making capability within minutes; weaker Nov Drama failures often extend helplessness or politics.
5. **Visual-first spectacle can carry sparse dialogue** when the vulnerable object and bond are readable (`2YkJl0KV3gs`). Dense dialogue is not inherently bad when it performs immediate dramatic action (`bHCI5XUSppM`).
6. **Format grammar differs by channel.** Nov Drama is vertical/mobile melodrama; Ironcrest/Starseed samples are landscape cinematic spectacle. Thumbnail, composition, pacing, and sound conclusions must therefore be segmented by format, not pooled blindly.
7. **Child/creature is a broad cross-channel mechanism but still not sufficient.** It works when vulnerability is paired with protection, bonding, progression, and payoff.
8. **Nonlinear openings can work when they are short and concrete.** `LU74Q4y3-Jk` uses transformation before “one day earlier”; the mystery is visible and emotionally simple, unlike nested abstract backstory.

## Falsification/caution

- These are hits or strong videos, not matched failures inside each channel yet. They cannot prove causality.
- Public views are affected by age, impressions, language market, packaging, and recommendation systems.
- Contact-sheet inspection samples moments across runtime; it is stronger than metadata-only analysis but weaker than continuous human viewing.
- Cross-channel findings must be separated by genre and format before being used as production rules.
