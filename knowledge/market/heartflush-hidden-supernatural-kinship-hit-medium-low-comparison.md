## Reusable production doctrine

1. A hidden-child plot is strongest when concealment is driven by credible custody, safety, or economic fear rather than avoidable misunderstanding.
2. Children need a concrete mission and collective decision-making, not just cuteness; their agency must have rules, limits, and consequences.
3. Supernatural transformation/power should create exposure risk. Rules such as “no shifting in public” generate suspense because children can break them.
4. Reveal kinship progressively. Each child or evidence layer should change adult knowledge, power, and strategy.
5. Use independent verification: birthmarks can start suspicion, but DNA, bloodline pressure, records, cameras, ledgers, witnesses, or physicians must carry consequence.
6. Wealth or biological fatherhood must not automatically override the caregiver. Money offered as replacement for children should be refused or repaired into support/respect.
7. The caregiver needs independent competence beyond parenting/romance, ideally thematically connected to the concealment plot.
8. Family/status prejudice is not resolved by lineage alone; material truth about abuse, theft, manipulation, or false accusation must also emerge.
9. Romance/reunion must remain chosen. Children can catalyze recognition but should not be treated as leverage that forces love.
10. After public vindication, dramatize secure ordinary belonging. Do not replace the children's emotional aftermath with an instant pregnancy/status epilogue.
11. Do not treat low-performing child-care films as craft failures when they are polished and coherent; isolate the exact discovery mechanism difference instead.
