## Comparative diagnosis

The hit and medium share Tales By Amy's core moral style: direct dialogue, explicit lessons, family mediation, female economic identity, public judgment, and closure through self-respect. Both are polished; the medium is not weakly written.

Observable hit differentiation:

1. The age-gap premise is a clearer high-contrast social question in title/thumbnail: viewers immediately know what is being judged.
2. The hit gives both sides of the judgment complexity: the older widower has grief history, the younger woman refuses money, adult children fear replacement, and the rival weaponizes social legitimacy.
3. The false-money accusation supplies a procedural middle act, so social judgment becomes evidence/action rather than only conversation.
4. The hit repeatedly returns to autonomous adult choice: Amy knows the costs and chooses; Kingsley does not excuse coercion through wealth or grief.
5. The medium has strong self-worth dialogue but is shorter, quieter, and more therapeutic; its central conflict is silence/body-shaming rather than a high-curiosity romantic taboo.

These are observable structural/packaging differences, not CTR/retention causation.

## Reusable doctrine — age-gap/autonomy romance

1. Both partners must be unambiguously adults; age difference is a social/power condition, not a fetish or excuse for coercion.
2. Financial/power asymmetry must be named. The younger partner should have independent judgment, work, refusal rights, and the ability to reject money or leave.
3. Love should be demonstrated through behavior—patience, respect, grief literacy, honesty, apology—not wealth spectacle.
4. Family opposition should contain legitimate grief/protection concerns, not only prejudice; the couple must answer concerns with transparency rather than demand instant acceptance.
5. False accusations or gold-digger framing require procedural evidence: transfer records, witnesses, timelines, account access, documents, or confessions.
6. Jealous rivals may be morally accountable, but the story should not imply only same-age love is legitimate; the rival fails because of manipulation, not age.
7. Consent and communication should continue after marriage; the wedding is not the end of power negotiation.
8. Pregnancy/children must not be required to prove love. If used, frame them as chosen family continuity, not validation for skeptics.
9. Endings should preserve the younger partner's independent identity/work and the couple's ordinary practices of respect, not merely win public approval.
10. Counterexample discipline: the medium body-image film is emotionally strong and professionally grounded; lower discovery is not weak-writing proof.
