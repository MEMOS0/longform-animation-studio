## Medium comparator compressed map — `BXEb8g0sopQ`

The shopkeeper opens with $10 and $3m debt. A fantasy merchant begs for water; ordinary bottled water becomes cross-world value. She then feeds 412 starving dwarfs as a loan with interest, requesting magical items ordinary people can use. The story expands through thrift armor, artifact auctions, portal logistics, dragon transport/protection/intelligence, and a trade alliance. By 55–57m she explicitly frames trade as alliance construction: dragons, dwarves, elves, humans, pirates and Spartans reduce the Blue Tower's ability to touch her. The ending is not simple wealth: she uses economics, logistics, and alliances to outnumber the antagonist's magic and prepares to march on the Blue Tower.

This is adjacent to constructive-restoration doctrine but distinct in emphasis: mundane retail inventory becomes diplomatic infrastructure. It validates the larger pattern that magic is strongest when converted into production, logistics, alliances, and obligations.

## Low comparator compressed map — `6TUtWHGCBxM`

The low film is a strong counterexample. It opens with unpaid wages and abusive antique fraud. Serena can hear antiques: a supposed imperial piece is factory-made, with “microwave safe” inside; print-grid lines expose a fake painting. She uses object testimony to identify hidden value, expose family fraud, reject relatives who abandoned her, and eventually hand national treasures to the country. The final inheritance is explicitly meant to let her do more good.

Why this is not a failure oracle: the mechanism is legible, the opening is fast, the thumbnail clearly visualizes object-talking, and the fraud/provenance logic is concrete. Observable differences are scope and conversion: the power mostly authenticates/extracts value and escalates revenge/wealth, whereas the hit converts magic into productive labor, water rights, food/medicine, community, child recovery, and institutional restoration. This difference is structural evidence, not proof of causality.

## Doctrine extracted — bounded magic must become livelihood infrastructure

1. Bind magic to explicit limits: wishes, refill cycles, scarcity, prohibitions, cost, or narrow domain.
2. Start with concrete dispossession or precarity: stolen estate, debt, wage theft, water access, hungry dependents.
3. Let magic create an input or opportunity, not the final result.
4. Require labor and operational learning: planting, selling, transport, negotiation, quality control, staffing, risk.
5. Solve resource conflict through documents, rights, audits, evidence, or procedure.
6. Scale through quality/scarcity/reputation/alliances rather than infinite conjuring.
7. Attach restoration to child/family/community agency, not only personal enrichment.
8. Use institutional evidence for accountability; magic should not replace law or audit.
9. End by refusing infinite accumulation and proving self-sufficiency, relationship repair, or stewardship.
