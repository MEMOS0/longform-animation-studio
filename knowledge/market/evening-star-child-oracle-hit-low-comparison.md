## What the hit does that the comparator does not sustain as cleanly

The hit is not merely “child knows the truth.” It builds a coherent psychological mechanism:

1. **Power is demonstrated as protection before explanation.** At 00:13 Chloe warns “You're going to die”; at 00:36 the death countdown becomes concrete; at 01:03–01:07 she explains that she sees a flash before someone gets hurt.
2. **Care precedes family reward.** At 01:26–01:30 Chloe denies saving Alexander only so he will help find her father: she did not want to watch him die.
3. **Usefulness is socially disruptive.** At 05:40–06:07 adults who called her a hero reclassify her as dangerous/monster after she throws a man and violates adult secrets.
4. **Identity is procedural.** DNA is not a single answer. Samples are tampered with, repeated, sealed, recorded, legally supervised, and combined with birthmarks, birthdays, hospital footage, payment trails, and a falsified death certificate.
5. **The antagonist attacks the evidence chain and the child.** Richard falsifies records, pays for the death certificate, kidnaps Chloe/grandmother, uses access badges, and arranges gunmen.
6. **Power is a wound adaptation.** At 55:22–56:22 Chloe fears losing usefulness when her strength becomes unstable; at 64:19–65:40 the countdown ends, strength/visions are gone, and she says she no longer needs visions because she knows the adults will come. “That's what home is.”

This mechanism was already integrated through `child_power_obsolescence`; no duplicate validator is needed.

## Early-low comparator evidence

`nw4JMSvyWJA` opens with a genuinely strong premise: Leo finds Elena at 00:00, calls Ken daddy at 00:23, a DNA test at 01:32–01:37 gives 99.99% parentage, and at 02:11–02:31 he claims to be from five years in the future, trying to fix time and save his parents' marriage. This falsifies any simple claim that the video failed because the opening is weak.

The observable structural difference is coherence and density:

- The first five minutes move from engagement-party shock to hospital proof to time-travel exposition to hiding the child from media, then into domestic bickering. The hook is strong, but several explanatory frames compete before one mechanism has been emotionally stabilized.
- Dialogue density is extremely high: 179–229 words/minute in the first five minutes and a 207.4 mean across the film. Much of the runtime is corporate/status explanation: stock, assets, scandal, frozen accounts, liquidation protocol, biometric scans, federal corporate fraud, offshore routing, Zurich, shell companies, and security grids.
- Leo sometimes functions as a matchmaker/commentator rather than an investigator whose ability has consistent rules. At 25:04 the “time lock” is introduced as if already understood; at 33:13–33:24 a hidden magnetic induction field/security protocol solves danger abruptly; at 37:04–37:14 Leo's silver cross suddenly supplies specific florist/bomb information.
- The ending does contain a real relational closure: at 39:11–39:27 Leo says the cold lonely future is gone, time does not tug at him anymore, and he is staying. This is not bad writing. The problem is that the closure is less causally seeded than Chloe's power-loss/home replacement because the time machinery has been entangled with arbitrary corporate techno-thriller escalation.

## Production doctrine refinement

The reusable mechanism is broader than “child predicts future”:

> A child-oracle family mystery works when the child's impossible knowledge is protective, socially costly, procedurally tested, and psychologically rooted in abandonment; the ending must replace supernatural vigilance with reliable human care.

Additional negative constraint from the comparator:

> Do not let the child oracle become a delivery device for corporate exposition. Every operational/techno-thriller complication must either threaten the child-family bond or be testable through the child's unstable knowledge rules.

## Validator decision

No new validator was added. The existing `child_power_obsolescence` validator already covers the core positive doctrine: demonstration before explanation, protective bond before biological proof, procedural evidence, child agency, power cost, power destabilization, love not payment for power, ordinary belonging closure, and sequel-hook ordering. This comparison adds evidence breadth and a same-channel counterexample rather than a separate overlapping machine gate.
