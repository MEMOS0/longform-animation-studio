## Core doctrine: betrayal-triggered restitution/vision-irony engine

A reusable supernatural melodrama engine emerges:

1. **A sensory or bodily sacrifice must be explicit and costly** — eyes, blood, heart, voice, memory, or another capacity is given to save beneficiaries.
2. **The sacrifice is governed by a conditional accountability mechanism** — betrayal triggers restitution and consequence; punishment is contractual, not arbitrary.
3. **Beneficiaries must vow protection before violating it** — their own promise becomes the standard by which they are judged.
4. **Restitution creates dramatic irony before safety** — restored sight/knowledge/power must be hidden or strategically managed so the audience sees judgment before the betrayers do.
5. **Caretaker history is inverted into resource entitlement** — beneficiaries reinterpret the protagonist’s voluntary sacrifice as permanent ownership.
6. **Bodily extraction escalates through excuses** — blood/heart/labor are taken because the protagonist “can survive it,” turning resilience into a consent violation.
7. **A public forced-choice test reveals the real hierarchy** — the betrayers must instinctively choose the false victim or their own interest in a way no later speech can undo.
8. **Consequence is a moral ledger made visible** — transformation, loss, or monstrous embodiment externalizes accumulated harm rather than serving as random spectacle.
9. **Late repentance must be exposed as self-preservation when instrumental** — apology motivated by curse-breaking or fear is not repair.
10. **The alternative partner must prove care before status** — rescue, respect for refusal, patience, and non-coercion come before identity/status reward.
11. **Identity reveal should follow a consent test** — the protagonist must be free to refuse or leave before learning that the partner is powerful.
12. **Closure separates accountability from romance** — betrayers receive consequence; the new bond receives trust only through chosen reciprocal vows.

## What not to copy

- Do not copy the literal demon, five vampire brothers, Morgana, Lucian, eye-sacrifice, or bat-transformation plot.
- Do not make sensory disability a decorative aesthetic. If blindness/deafness/voice loss is used, it must alter knowledge, testimony credibility, vulnerability, and power.
- Do not let the powerful alternative partner “win” the heroine as a reward. He must respect refusal before status is revealed or accepted.
- Do not treat regeneration/healing as consent. “It will grow back” is an antagonist excuse, not a neutral fact.
- Do not use forgiveness as a magic key that the heroine owes. If forgiveness releases a curse, the story must recognize the coercion implicit in that mechanism.
- Do not make female rivalry simple misogynistic caricature. A false-victim manipulator can work, but original production should give her legible status/economic motives rather than “women are naturally deceitful.”
- Do not equate monstrous appearance with evil as a general rule. In this mechanism, transformation is legible because it executes a specific pact after repeated choices, not because monsters are inherently bad.

## Production-transfer guidance

Use this engine when the story needs a long-form moral test rather than a simple revenge plot. The central pleasure is not “the heroine becomes strong”; it is “the world slowly catches up to a judgment the audience already understands.” Structure acts as repeated opportunities for betrayers to choose repair. Each refusal deepens the ledger. Let spectacle arrive after the audience can decode what every transformation means.

For original development, change the domain while preserving mechanics: memory sacrifice in a sci-fi colony, voice sacrifice in a musical dynasty, healing labor in a hospital empire, magical sight in a court intrigue, or economic credit sacrifice in a family business. The stronger the protagonist’s restored perception must be hidden, the more dramatic irony the middle episodes generate.
