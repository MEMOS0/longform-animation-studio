# Craft research synthesis: story, retention, direction, and sound

Status: living research note. These are craft priors, not Nov Drama-specific causal findings. Every rule must be retested against archive evidence and project validation artifacts.

## Source base

## What the evidence supports carefully

### 1. A dramatic question is an attention engine, not a decorative hook

The Frontiers YouTube study found relationships between popularity and five storytelling components: dramatic question, insight, moments of change, emotional arousal, and status of story. This was science-video research, so it does not directly prove fantasy melodrama behavior. It still supports a general mechanism: viewers stay when the story creates a meaningful uncertainty and periodically pays it with change or insight.

Operational translation:

- Every project needs a visible central dramatic question, not just a premise.
- Every act should create, sharpen, complicate, or partially answer a question.
- A scene that neither changes power nor deepens a question is retention risk.
- “What happens next?” is weaker than “Will this emotionally legible character gain/lose the belonging, safety, recognition, or justice we now care about?”

### 2. The opening is disproportionately dangerous

The VidCC analysis reports that in its sample the first 30 seconds were a small fraction of runtime but accounted for a disproportionate share of total viewer loss, and early retention strongly correlated with average retention. The sample is self-selected public retention graphs and mostly shorter long-form videos, so we must not import exact percentages into Nov Drama. Still, the directional prior is strong: opening confusion is expensive.

Operational translation:

- The first frame must already be emotionally readable.
- The viewer should know who to care about, what is wrong, and why the fantasy premise matters before dense mythology arrives.
- Proper nouns, councils, bloodline rules, and world mechanics should be delayed or dramatized through a concrete emotional problem.
- The first spectacle should clarify the promise, not merely display production value.

### 3. Emotional arousal works best when tied to moral/relational stakes

Emotional arousal in the research does not mean constant shouting or cruelty. It means the audience feels something. Unrelenting extremity can flatten into noise. Wholesome relief, protection, recognition, and belonging can be as activating as danger when contrast is strong.

Operational translation:

- Alternate pressure and care. Humiliation lands harder when the audience has seen vulnerability; rescue lands harder when the wound is concrete.
- Do not confuse intensity with volume, violence, or sexual coercion.
- Track minute-by-minute intended emotion. If the same emotion persists too long without new information or power change, the scene is probably flat even if events are loud.

### 4. Storyboard clarity precedes visual beauty

The animation storyboarding source emphasizes rough thumbnails as a way to solve sequencing, composition, framing, staging, and transitions before polish. Its practical standard is clarity: a casual observer should understand the action and emotion.

Operational translation:

- Do not evaluate a board only for beauty. Evaluate whether emotion, geography, eyelines, props, and power relationships read instantly.
- Close-ups are for emotional turns and decisions; wide shots are for spatial power, transformation scale, group hierarchy, and irreversible action.
- AI-generated video especially needs constraints: hand/prop continuity, character scale, costume state, direction of movement, and consistent emotional expression.

### 5. Sound is structure, not decoration

The sound-design sources emphasize that music carries emotional undertone, effects/Foley ground the body and world, atmospheres create place, and dialogue carries relationships. They also emphasize contrast and the power of removing sound.

Operational translation:

- A scene can fail visually yet still work if sound preserves emotional continuity; the inverse is also true.
- Do not wallpaper every second with music. Use silence or reduced texture before major reversals, intimate admissions, or threat realization.
- Sound bridges can soften hard transitions; abrupt silence can make a betrayal or transformation feel irreversible.
- Creature design is partly sonic. A dragon/wolf/vampire transformation needs body weight, air movement, material scale, and emotional intention—not just a generic roar.

## Studio heuristics derived from the research

These are hypotheses to test against market evidence:

1. **The first minute should contain a wound plus a promise.** Example mechanism: visible rejection + glimpse of hidden power/protector.
2. **Every 2–5 minutes needs a meaningful turn.** Not necessarily a twist; a relationship, power balance, information state, or emotional cost must change.
3. **The middle fails when it repeats the same injury.** Repetition is only safe when each repetition escalates cost, reveals capability, or changes alliances.
4. **Revenge is weaker as a terminal emotion than belonging/recognition.** Justice can satisfy the plot, but audience warmth often comes from protection, acceptance, family, or earned love.
5. **Dense mythology is a tax.** Spend it only after the viewer is emotionally invested.
6. **Spectacle must be causal.** A transformation or power display matters when it changes what characters can do, what they risk, or how others must respond.
7. **Mobile-native vertical framing prioritizes faces and relational blocking.** Emotional legibility usually outranks ornamental environment detail.
8. **The edit should protect comprehension before rhythm.** Fast cutting cannot rescue unclear staging or unclear dialogue.


## Deep craft addendum: character psychology, dialogue function, direction, and sound

These are operating standards for original writing. They are craft heuristics sharpened by the Nov Drama evidence base, not claims that any single rule causes views.

### Character psychology matrix

For every major character, define these fields before writing scenes:

1. **Public mask:** what the character wants others to believe.
2. **Private wound:** what pain made the mask necessary.
3. **Misbelief:** the false rule they use to survive (“love makes me weak,” “obedience keeps me safe,” “power is the only protection”).
4. **Defensive strategy:** submission, control, perfectionism, seduction, violence, caretaking, withdrawal, humor, or competence.
5. **Contradiction:** where their behavior conflicts with their stated values. Contradictions create watchable interiority.
6. **Moral line:** what they will not do even under pressure. A protector without a moral line becomes coercion; a victim without one can become passive suffering.
7. **Agency mechanism:** what choices they can make now, before gaining power. Agency is not “being strong”; it is making meaningful choices under constraint.
8. **Attachment pattern:** whom they trust too quickly, resist trusting, test, protect, or punish.
9. **Change proof:** the behavior at the end that would have been impossible at the beginning.

Weak melodrama often gives characters cruelty or victimhood without a psychological economy. Strong melodrama makes cruelty costly, protection morally bounded, and transformation visible in choices rather than narration.

### Dialogue function discipline

Every important line should perform at least one dramatic action:

- attack, defend, beg, test, seduce, threaten, confess, conceal, negotiate, comfort, accuse, invite, reject, or transfer power.

If a line only explains background, ask whether an image, object, action, or interruption can carry the information instead. Exposition is safest when it is incomplete, motivated by conflict, or delivered because a character needs something right now.

Useful tests:

- **Subtext test:** what is the character afraid to say directly?
- **Status test:** who gains or loses status during the exchange?
- **Interruption test:** what irreversible action can prevent the explanation from becoming a lecture?
- **Question test:** does the scene open or sharpen a question the audience wants answered?
- **Silence test:** would a look, pause, or physical reaction carry the emotion better than another sentence?

Do not equate fewer words with better writing. The failure pattern measured in Nov Drama is not sentence length; it is explanation before emotional grounding.

### Direction and shot-level emotional logic

For each scene, write the intended emotional transition in one sentence: “viewer enters feeling X and leaves feeling Y because Z changed.” Then choose shots to serve that transition.

Operational rules:

1. **Open on the most legible emotional fact**, not on ornamental environment unless the environment itself is the threat.
2. **Use close-ups for decision, realization, shame, tenderness, and moral injury.** Use wides for hierarchy, pursuit, transformation scale, and public humiliation.
3. **Reaction shots are information.** In melodrama, the face receiving cruelty or care often matters more than the person delivering it.
4. **Protect eyelines and relational blocking.** A tired mobile viewer must know who dominates, who is trapped, who is protecting, and who is watching.
5. **Treat objects as continuity anchors.** A stuffed animal, egg, ring, scar, contract, food bowl, or child’s drawing can carry memory better than repeated dialogue.
6. **Do not spend expensive spectacle on low-information scenes.** The cost of AI consistency errors rises with crowd scenes, action, transformations, animals, hands, props, and multi-character interaction.
7. **Vertical/mobile framing favors emotional composition.** Faces, hands, children/creatures, and threat/protection geometry must remain readable in portrait.

### Sound-design discipline

A professional sound pass should separately evaluate:

- **Dialogue intelligibility:** voices must remain clear over music and effects.
- **Emotional score function:** music should mark change, longing, danger, warmth, or release rather than fill silence uniformly.
- **Foley/body grounding:** footsteps, cloth, breath, impact, doors, creature weight, and environmental texture make AI images feel embodied.
- **Silence/reduced texture:** use restraint before confessions, reversals, deaths, recognitions, or transformation completion.
- **Motifs:** assign recurring sound identities to belonging, threat, transformation, family, or power. Do not rely only on visual motifs.
- **Transitions:** sound bridges can preserve momentum across time jumps; hard silence can make betrayal or realization feel irreversible.

Public Nov Drama comments repeatedly complain about loud music, missing sound, sudden cuts, censorship blur, and watermark-like overlays. Treat these as release-blocking defects even when their causal effect on views cannot be measured.

### Retention loop annotation

For each 5-minute block of a draft or animatic, annotate:

- question opened or sharpened
- partial answer or reversal delivered
- new cost or complication
- relief/warmth/wonder beat
- character choice
- visual novelty
- emotional polarity entering and leaving the block

If a block opens no question, answers nothing, changes no power relationship, and repeats the same emotion, compress or delete it. Retention is not “constant excitement”; it is controlled alternation of uncertainty, cost, agency, relief, and payoff.

## Measurement implications for this repository

The media-analysis system should track:

- time of first visible wound
- time of first concrete rejection/injustice
- time of first fantasy/identity promise
- time of first care/protection/belonging beat
- dialogue density per minute
- shot-boundary and motion distribution
- close-up/wide balance (requires agent annotation; not yet automated reliably)
- emotional polarity transitions (requires agent annotation)
- sound intensity/onsets/silence ratio
- proper-noun/mythology density in dialogue (requires NLP implementation)
- repeated humiliation count without agency progression (requires scene annotation)

## Evidence boundaries

- The academic YouTube study is about science communication, not fantasy drama.
- The retention study is observational and self-selected; use directionally.
- Craft books are expert heuristics, not controlled evidence.
- Nov Drama archive outcomes are necessary but insufficient evidence. The strongest conclusions will come from converging evidence across full-video analysis, comments, packaging comparisons, and replication in other channels.
