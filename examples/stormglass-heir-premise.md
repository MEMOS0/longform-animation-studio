# Original premise self-test — working title: Stormglass Heir

This is a mechanism-level original test case, not a copy of any Nov Drama plot.

## Promise

Title candidates:

1. They Burned the Bellmaker’s Mute Daughter… Unaware Her Glass Dragon Could Stop the Storm
2. The Guild Threw Away the Bellmaker’s Girl… Then Her Dragon Child Revealed the Stolen Crown
3. She Was Too Weak to Ring the Storm Bell… Until Her Glass Dragon Hatched the Queen’s Heir

Logline: A mute bellmaker’s daughter, blamed for a storm that destroyed her floating city, secretly raises a fragile glass dragon whose sound can calm lightning; when the guild discovers the creature is bonded to the missing heir of the storm throne, she must choose between hiding her childlike dragon companion or ringing the forbidden bell that will expose them both.

## Emotional engine

- Wound: The protagonist was mute after watching her mother die ringing the storm bell; the city treats her as cursed.
- Want: To prove she is not a curse and protect the glass dragon hatchling she rescued.
- Need: To accept that her voice is not what makes her powerful; her care, listening, and courage are.
- Injustice visible in first minute: Guild soldiers publicly seize her dead mother's bell hammer and accuse her of causing the storm.
- Protective counterforce: The glass dragon is small, frightened, transparent, and physically vulnerable, but responds to her tapping as language.
- Secret/power asymmetry: The dragon is not merely a pet; it is the storm throne's living key, bonded to the missing royal heir.
- Cost of failure: If she rings the bell, the guild learns exactly where she and the dragon are; if she does not, the city drowns.
- Belonging payoff: The city does not merely fear her power; the bellmakers, fishermen, orphan children, and the displaced queen publicly accept her as guardian of a new storm covenant.

## Opening test

- 0–10 seconds: A glass egg cracks in the protagonist's hands during a lightning strike; inside, a translucent dragonet reflects her terrified face.
- 10–60 seconds: Soldiers hammer on the workshop door; she hides the dragon inside a hollow bell while they accuse her of causing the storm.
- 1–5 minutes: The dragonet imitates her tapping and calms a small lightning arc, proving the fantasy promise. A child orphan sees them and chooses to help rather than expose them. The guild master's son publicly takes credit for her mother's bell design.
- Dramatic question: Can a girl the city believes is cursed protect the creature that proves she is not?

## Why this uses evidence mechanisms rather than copying

- Uses early creature visibility like the strongest dragon hits, but the creature is a sound/glass/storm creature with different rules and visual identity.
- Uses wound → protection → belonging escalation rather than copying adoption/royalty scenes.
- Uses early partial payoff: the creature visibly works in the opening and the crown/heir stakes emerge before minute 15.
- Avoids opening council politics, ritual reports, sexual coercion, or generic court selection.
- Ending is not only revenge: the guild lie is exposed early enough to escalate, while final resolution centers on public belonging and child/creature safety.

## First-15-minute escalation draft

1. 0–5 min: egg hatches, accusation, hiding, first sound-control proof.
2. 5–8 min: guild auctioneers prepare to sell the mother's bell hammer; protagonist chooses theft rather than passive mourning.
3. 8–11 min: dragonet reveals a memory-image inside glass: her mother did not fail; she was silenced by the guild master.
4. 11–15 min: royal heir child appears as a fugitive, not as exposition. The dragonet recognizes the child's storm mark. The first public consequence lands: the lying guild son is humiliated when the bell rejects his hand.

## Failure risks and mitigations

- Risk: hidden-identity mythology becomes abstract. Mitigation: every revelation appears through sound, glass memory, bell behavior, or child/creature action.
- Risk: mute protagonist reduces dialogue accessibility. Mitigation: tapping rhythm, subtitles-as-visual-design, expressive face closeups, and selective voiceover only when emotionally necessary.
- Risk: cute creature becomes decorative. Mitigation: dragonet makes choices, causes trouble, sacrifices safety, and changes plot outcomes.
- Risk: guild villainy becomes repetitive humiliation. Mitigation: each attack produces a clue, ally, capability, or irreversible decision.

- `BeX5LHLqOEI`: animal-child imagery is insufficient when opening uses report/court machinery.
- `gkRXl9lDReY`: mythology is unnecessary if injustice is instantly legible; therefore this premise must justify its fantasy layer visually.
