# Sixth-pass breakout thumbnail analysis

Images in this folder are reference artifacts for mechanism study, not assets to copy. Performance metadata is in `manifest.json`. We do not have CTR, impressions, or retention; these are design-quality observations, not causal claims.

## `or5RI5v_lHw` — DRAMORA compressed immigrant melodrama

Visual mechanics:

- **Relational close-up:** the abandoned wife and customer/billionaire occupy most of the frame, with eye contact and tearful vulnerability immediately legible.
- **Place/object proof:** the `Indian Food` cart, West 34th St sign, snow, Empire State silhouette, American flag, and Manhattan Grill signage compress immigrant displacement, street labor, winter vulnerability, and hidden wealth into one image.
- **Class contrast without clutter:** the woman’s grey coat/yellow scarf and food cart sit against the warm luxury restaurant and the man’s dark expensive coat.
- **Text role:** “ABANDONED IN AMERICA!” and “BILLIONAIRE CUSTOMER!” are large and redundant with the title, but they make the premise readable at feed size. The red/yellow paint-stroke blocks sacrifice elegance for mobile clarity.
- **Mobile readability:** strongest elements are the two faces, yellow scarf, food cart label, and huge text. Secondary details disappear but do not carry the core meaning.
- **Mechanic:** vulnerable worker + intimate customer relation + place-specific survival + hidden-status promise.

Risk: heavy text explains rather than creates paradox; still, the face/object/place system carries emotional specificity.

## `TKUd7hWPMD8` — Karma Keeps Score floor/table social-proof melodrama

Visual mechanics:

- **Exact story mechanism in one frame:** the maid sits on the floor with food, the child kneels beside her, and the fiancée points down from a chair. No viewer needs genre knowledge to understand the injustice.
- **Spatial status grammar:** floor vs chair vs dining table is the entire power system. The child physically crossing that boundary creates curiosity and moral warmth.
- **Public shame layer:** guests in the background laugh or film with phones, turning private humiliation into social spectacle. The male silhouette in the doorway suggests incoming authority/reckoning without overcrowding the foreground.
- **Emotional triangle:** maid’s hurt face, child’s protective innocence, fiancée’s accusatory gesture. Each role is readable through body position before text.
- **Palette/composition:** warm luxury dining room and chandelier establish wealth; purple antagonist dress contrasts with maid’s muted green uniform and child’s soft blue dress. The floor reflection literally separates status levels.
- **Text restraint:** “NOT AT MY TABLE” is short, yellow, and bottom-left. It names the rule but does not explain the whole plot; the image carries the story.
- **Mobile readability:** the three foreground figures and pointing gesture survive shrinkage. Background phones/guests enrich but are not required.

This is a reference-grade example of thumbnail-image alignment: the thumbnail does not merely decorate the title; it visualizes the exact moral test and relational reversal engine.

## Comparative rule distilled

- For social melodrama, the strongest thumbnail often stages the **status rule being violated or enforced** rather than showing generic attractive faces.
- A child/innocent ally is especially powerful when physically crossing a class boundary.
- Text should name a short rule or paradox; composition should show who is harmed, who has power, and who crosses the line.
- Concrete objects/spaces (food cart, floor, table, doorway, phone) outperform abstract luxury backgrounds because they become proof of the story mechanism.
