## Cross-channel thumbnail rules added to Thumbnail Intelligence

1. **Relational scale beats anonymous scale.** A giant looking tenderly at a baby is stronger evidence than monsters/mechas fighting at equivalent visual quality.
2. **The vulnerable anchor must be large enough to emotionally read.** Tiny humans behind monsters do not create the same protective contract.
3. **A thumbnail can carry paradox without text.** Queen + human baby + dragon baby communicates the premise visually; text then adds tension rather than explanation.
4. **Battle complexity is not emotional clarity.** Multiple factions, explosions, and premise captions can reduce mobile hierarchy.
5. **Franchise/part labels can create friction.** “Part II” should not be assumed safe for discovery unless the channel audience is already trained.
6. **Do not declare very new uploads failures.** `Lh2wgnXz7uA` is unresolved because age-adjustment is unstable after one day.
7. **Do not infer CTR.** These are correlations between packaging and public discovery outcomes, not measured click causality.

## Production use

When generating original thumbnails:

- start with one vulnerable/protected anchor and one protective or threatening counterforce;
- make the emotional relationship readable before adding genre scale;
- use text to resolve the title/image division of labor, not to compensate for an emotionally unclear image;
- produce one “paradox image” concept, one “relational protection” concept, and one “scale/spectacle” concept, then critique all three at feed size;
- exclude off-format/tutorial uploads from film packaging comparisons.
